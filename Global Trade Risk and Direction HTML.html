Lens 2 — forward-looking

Growth direction model

A Gradient Boosting regressor predicts each corridor's next-year trade growth from its own history, shipping conditions, commodity prices, disruption exposure and industry vulnerability — evaluated on a strict time-based holdout (train ≤2019, validate 2020–21, test 2022–23).

Critical findings

What actually held up under scrutiny

Four findings that changed how we'd describe this system to a stakeholder — including one that determines how the two lenses above should, and should not, be used together.

Do the two lenses agree?

Structural exposure ≠ growth direction

We tested whether corridors flagged as structurally "high risk" in a given year actually go on to contract the following year, using the full historical panel where the outcome is already known.

Nearly independent signals, by design and by measurement

The overlap above is drawn to scale with the data: a corridor's structural-exposure score tells you almost nothing about which direction its trade will move next year, and vice versa. That's not a flaw in either framework — they're built to answer different questions.
